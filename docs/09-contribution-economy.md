# 09. Contribution Economy

The system's throughput depends on people who are not paid employees. This document defines
what earns value, how it is recorded, and how it resists being farmed.

## 1. The ledger

`points_ledger` is append-only. No mutable score column exists anywhere in the schema.
Balances and leaderboards are derived by aggregation. (ADR-0019)

Every row carries an actor, a signed amount, a reason code, and a subject reference. Reversals
are compensating negative rows carrying `reverses_entry_id`, never deletions or updates. History
survives and disputes are auditable, which matters because a contributor who believes they were
cheated will say so publicly.

| Reason code | Trigger | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| `submission.provisional` | Peer review grants provisional status | Base, bounty-multiplied |
| `submission.confirmed` | Operator adjudication confirms | Remainder |
| `submission.reversed` | Adjudication rejects, or duplicate detected | Negative, matching the original |
| `review.resolved` | A review task the reviewer answered reaches quorum | Small, fixed |
| `review.reversed` | Adjudication contradicts the resolved answer | Negative |
| `lexicon.first_resolution` | A T1 answer creates a new lexicon entry | Elevated |

All amounts come from `tuning.json`. Retuning must never require a deploy. (ADR-0021)

## 2. Award on acceptance, not submission

Points are granted when an observation passes integrity checks and enters the corpus, not when
a photo lands. Stated to contributors in plain language at signup.

The problem this creates is latency: operator adjudication can take days, and a contributor
who sees nothing after submitting will stop. `provisional` solves it. Peer review grants
provisional status within minutes, the ledger entry is written immediately, and final
adjudication either confirms the remainder or writes a reversal. (ADR-0050)

Reversals are visible to the contributor with their reason. A silent clawback is worse than no
clawback.

## 3. Reward marginal information value

The central design decision. Reward tracks **how much the corpus did not already know**, not
how many things were submitted. (ADR-0020)

If points scale with raw count, a leaderboard is an instruction to farm the nearest store with
the fewest items, and contributors will follow it precisely. Coverage would concentrate exactly
where it is already dense.

Value is computable:

```
value = base
      * staleness_factor(branch, product)     -- how old the newest observation in that cell is
      * scarcity_factor(branch, product)      -- how few independent contributors cover it
      * bounty_multiplier(active bounties)
```

A cell with no observation is worth many times the fifth observation of the same product at the
same branch today.

## 4. Bounties

`bounties` targets a branch, a product, a category, or a combination, with a multiplier and an
expiry.

| Reason | Generated by |
|---|---|
| `empty_cell` | Scheduled sweep over basket coverage |
| `stale_cell` | Scheduled sweep against the staleness window |
| `campaign` | Operator, for a specific push |

Bounties turn the leaderboard from a noise generator into a coverage-optimisation mechanism.
They are also the main lever available when index coverage falls below its publication floor.

Two constraints. Bounties must not be so concentrated that they distort the sample beyond what
ADR-0080 already discloses, since bounty-directed collection is named there as a validity
threat. And overlapping bounties on one cell do not stack multiplicatively; the highest single
multiplier applies.

## 5. Leaderboards

Derived, never stored. Windowed (weekly, monthly) rather than all-time, because an all-time
board is unwinnable for a newcomer and therefore demotivating for exactly the people you need.

Boards rank on **value contributed**, not submission count, for the reasons in section 3.

Leaderboards show display names, never phone numbers, and never link a board entry to a specific
submission. Doing so would defeat the reviewer anonymity in ADR-0051 by a different route.

## 6. Reviewing is rewarded, but weakly

Review earns points, but small fixed amounts, and only when the task reaches quorum with the
reviewer in agreement. Reviewers are scored on eventual agreement, never on volume, and
`review_weight` decays for indiscriminate approval. (ADR-0049)

The failure mode being avoided: a reviewer who approves everything as fast as possible earns
the most, and peer review becomes a rubber stamp that launders bad data.

Honeypots make this work from a contributor's first session rather than their fiftieth, because
they give an immediate accuracy signal without waiting for corroboration. (ADR-0061)

## 7. Anti-farming

| Vector | Defence |
|---|---|
| Volume farming | Value-weighted rewards (section 3) |
| Nearest-store farming | Staleness and scarcity factors |
| Resubmitting old receipts | Fingerprint dedupe; recency window on reward eligibility |
| Duplicate images | Perceptual hash |
| Rubber-stamp reviewing | Honeypots, weight decay, agreement-based scoring |
| Multi-accounting | Phone OTP raises the cost; device fingerprint feeds the independence rules |
| Collusion | Independence rules on referral, device and mutual review history (ADR-0048) |

Rate monitoring is a signal, not a rule. A contributor suddenly submitting forty receipts in a
week may be farming or may have just discovered the app. It routes to review, it does not block.

## 8. Payouts are outside the system

The ledger records value earned. Converting points to money is an operational process performed
by a human, not a platform feature.

This is deliberate. Payouts require a legal entity and a payment rail, and TRNC's position makes
both a separate track. Building a payout system against an unresolved rail would encode
assumptions that are likely wrong.

What the ledger must therefore support is export: a per-contributor, per-period statement of
confirmed value, with reversals applied, that a human can act on. That is a report, not a
feature.

The consequence to state plainly to any paid contributor: payment is for **accepted and novel**
contributions, never for submissions. Fingerprint and phash dedupe are what make that promise
enforceable rather than aspirational.

## 9. Erasure

An erased contributor's ledger entries survive, severed to the shared tombstone. (ADR-0084)

They are excluded from leaderboards. Aggregate totals remain correct because the entries are
retained, which is the point of severing rather than deleting.

Payout obligations accrued before erasure are settled outside the system, since the ledger no
longer identifies the person.
